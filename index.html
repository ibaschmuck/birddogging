The Standards

Nothing here is unreasonable.
It is just written down.

These are the standards Terry enforces on site. They are not negotiated at the start of the job and they are not negotiated at the end of it.

Painting

  • Rolled. Never sprayed. Spraying is not painting, it is aiming.
  • Five coats minimum, regardless of what the can claims about coverage.
  • Cut in by hand. Tape is a confession.
  • Cans opened, stirred, and thinned in Terry’s presence or not at all.

Flooring & Carpet

  • Subfloor is inspected before one square foot is covered.
  • Seams fall away from the doorway and away from the window.
  • Power stretcher. A knee kicker is a tool for corners, not for rooms.
  • Transitions sit flush or they come back up the same day.

Concrete & Masonry

  • Forms stay twenty‑eight hours. Not nineteen. Not “overnight.”
  • Rebar is tied, not laid on the dirt and poured around.
  • Edges troweled by hand after the machine has done what it can.
  • No water added to the mix at the truck. Terry watches the truck.

Roofing & Siding

  • Six nails per shingle. Four is a manufacturer’s minimum, not a plan.
  • Drip edge under the underlayment at the eave, over it at the rake.
  • Old layer comes off. It does not get a second layer laid over it.
  • Valleys are woven or open, and either way Terry sees them before they close.

Plumbing & Electrical

  • Code minimum is a floor, not a goal.
  • Joints soldered and wiped, then wiped again.
  • Every box gets six inches of slack for whoever comes after.
  • Wire nuts are tugged. In front of Terry. Every one of them.

The Proprietor

Forty years of walking
onto other people’s job sites.

Terry spent four decades in project management — schedules, subcontractors, punch lists, and the particular silence that falls over a crew when the man with the clipboard comes through the door.

He retired. He did not stop. Birddogging is what happened next, and it exists for one reason: you cannot be in two places, and the work is only as good as whoever is standing there while it happens.

For twenty percent of your project cost, Terry will be in your house from the first truck to the last, and the job will be done the way it was done in the good old days — rolled, cured, stretched, nailed, and finished.
